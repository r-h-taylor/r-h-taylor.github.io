CS_F2012_10 · Codes and Standards, Fall 2012 · §4.p2

Used to illustrate the social-criterion factor: few people exposed but high economic value, so the regulatory standard accepts higher failure probability than for a dam or bridge. References "Hurricane Cino [?]" — captioner-garbled, unresolved storm name.

What it says is, if you're looking at something that a lot of people would be exposed to a failure of, like a dam, you want to minimize even further your risk. If you have 2,000 people on a bridge, it's not like my tire blowing up because it's a hot day and I was in park at the right location. Now these factors — when we're looking down here for towers, masts, and offshore structures — I think the offshore structure is really true. When Hurricane Cino [?] or some of the Gulf storms come in, it's not atypical to have a whole platform just collapsing. You hope that before they do, they're able to shut the well. But the basic idea is that it's out there — there are very few people exposed to it. It's worth a lot of money, but it becomes more of an economical decision rather than a people-safety decision.
