`Boston Molasses Flood`

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

CS_F2012_14 · Codes and Standards, Fall 2012 · §1.p3

Cited briefly as a historical example of why large stored-liquid structures in populated areas need code-governed design. The speaker explicitly distinguishes this from the Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code lineage but uses it for the same "structures near populations need oversight" argument.

The pressure vessel example is another one — when we had the molasses disaster in Boston a very long time ago. That led people to realize we can't have structures storing large quantities of liquid in a city without having any control over how it's designed, how strong it is, and what would happen if it fails.
