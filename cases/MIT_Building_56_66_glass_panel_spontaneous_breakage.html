MIT Building 56/66 glass panel spontaneous breakage

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

MSE_F2016_10 · Materials Selection, Fall 2016 · §5.p1

Substitute walked in from the T and noticed a fractured glass panel on building 56 (or 66). Facilities reported this was not the first panel to break in that location. The morning of the lecture, crews were replacing multiple panels — not just the broken one. Treated as a live, on-campus illustration of how thermal stress, installation defects, or inclusions can cause spontaneous fracture, and as a hint at the cost and litigation that follows.

But if they don't do it properly, you can get glass fracture. I want to pose this question: what do you think are some reasons for spontaneous glass fracture? I was walking in the other day from the T over by building 56, and what did I see when I looked up — I didn't throw a rock at it — but a glass panel had fractured. The facilities guys said, we have another panel that broke. It's not the first one. That raises questions about how the glass was made, things like that. What do you think are some reasons a panel just breaks one day?
