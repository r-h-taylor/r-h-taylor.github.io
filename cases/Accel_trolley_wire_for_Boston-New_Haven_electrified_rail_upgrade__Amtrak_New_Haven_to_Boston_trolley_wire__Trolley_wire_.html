MSE_F2017_01 · Materials Selection and Economics, Fall 2017 · §3.p4

[Tom holds up a copper tensile specimen.] Now there are cases where you'd like to have a fine-grained material for strength. This is copper, it's actually copper with just a little bit of silver in it, only two-tenths of a percent or a tenth of a percent to strengthen it, but it had very large grain size. I was given all of 48 hours to go to New York where they were making this, and they were going to draw it into wire for the Northeast extension. If you've taken Amtrak and the Acela train between New Haven and Boston, they were putting in the trolley wire. This is the overhead conductor that carries the electric current. You need the copper for wear resistance, corrosion resistance, electrical conductivity, but it also has to be a structural material, it has to be strong to be able to be strung along a couple hundred miles.
