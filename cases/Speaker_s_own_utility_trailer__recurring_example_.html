CS_F2012_10 · Codes and Standards, Fall 2012 · §1.p1

The lecturer's personal trailer, used in the prior session as a worked example of worst-case load design (lifting under highway wind pressure) and weld placement away from high-stress regions. Referenced three times in this lecture; not developed further.

We'll still talk about the factor of safety, but we'll use a new idea — statistics — to try to explain how we do things. The first time we talked about the definition and discussed that a safety factor of four was not really what we were doing. Yesterday we looked more closely at demand and capacity, and I gave some examples on designing for the worst-case load when I was talking about my trailer — that you want to be able to lift it up with the wind pressure from driving and still have everything strong enough. And then we talked about the little ATV, discussing that you don't have to put welds all around. It's still going to be strong enough in most cases.
