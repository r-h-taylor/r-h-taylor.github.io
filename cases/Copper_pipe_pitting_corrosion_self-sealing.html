Copper pipe pitting corrosion self-sealing

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

CS_F2012_10 · Codes and Standards, Fall 2012 · §2.p5

Brief aside within the rubber-vessel discussion. Hot-water copper pipes pit through wall thickness, then the dissolved salts and organics in the water deposit at the pit and seal it; failure presents as a white stain rather than as a leak. Used to extend the lesson that self-sealing defects can defeat inspection.

Now when you come up with all of this, you're assuming that the crack is not going to seal itself. And that wasn't the case with this vessel, because of that rubber membrane. We have projects where we see copper pipes that have holes that go all the way through the thickness by pitting corrosion, and they seal themselves. If it's hot water, you have a little bit of flow. All the salts, and maybe some organics from the water, just come through the crack and seal it. So what are you going to do about this? Are you going to also be inspecting for stain? Because most of the time it is visible, but as a stain — a white stain, as opposed to a leak.
