Rhodesian chrome embargo

Appears in 2 lectures.

Appearances across the corpus

SMS_F2014_01 · Structural Materials Selection, Fall 2014 · §8.p14

Forty-year-old example of political externality. Rhodesian chrome ore is the world's best; embargo on it could be defeated by laundering through other countries, but quality was distinctive enough that buyers knew the source.

Go back forty years ago — Rhodesian chrome, in Africa. Chrome ore — Rhodesian chrome ore is the best in the world. We can get chromium from a lot of other sources, but none of it's as good and as economical as Rhodesian chrome. They were trying — same type of thing we're trying to do in the Ukraine right now — politically, get the former Soviets to do what they should be doing, in our opinion. They tried to have an embargo on Rhodesian chrome, except you could always tell it was very decent chrome. They'd launder it through some other country, but no one else had that kind of chrome ore, so they knew they were really selling Rhodesian chrome.

SMS_F2013_01 · Structural Materials Selection, Fall 2013 · §6.p4

Political externality. The world's best chrome ore subjected to embargo during the civil war. Tom notes it's "a lot easier to detect Rhodesian chrome than blood diamonds" — visual identification rather than impurity analysis.

Before that, about the time you were born, there was Rhodesian chrome. Anybody heard of Rhodesia? Rhodesia was an east African nation just north of South Africa, and they have the world's best chrome ore. It's so good you can't reproduce it. It's the most economical. But they were having a civil war in Rhodesia, and the rest of the world was trying to pressure them, just like we're trying to pressure Syria right now because of their civil war. So they put an embargo on Rhodesian chrome. It's a lot easier to detect Rhodesian chrome than it is blood diamonds. Blood diamonds you've got to go in with some pretty sophisticated instrument. Rhodesian chrome you can sort of look at.
