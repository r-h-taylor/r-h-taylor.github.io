Hydrogen-induced cracking in martensitic stainless steels

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

WM_S2014_10 · Welding Metallurgy, Spring 2014 · §2.p1

Primary cracking concern in welding martensitic alloys. Addressed via preheat (400–600°F), low-hydrogen practice, and post-weld heat treatment.

Some things to consider when you're welding a martensitic stainless steel alloy. It's recommended that you preheat all the time when you're welding martensitic alloys. It's not absolutely required if you've got low carbon — below about 0.1 weight percent. The preheat that's recommended is about 400 to 600 degrees F. The reason is, it's a martensitic alloy, you've got to worry about hydrogen cracking, and the preheat will help minimize that. Post weld heat treatment is often also used. Sometimes you use an austenitic filler material, and in that case the post weld heat treatment is not required. But if you've used a matching or other filler material and you've got a high-stress weldment, that post weld heat treatment is a good idea to stress relieve and to temper the structure.
