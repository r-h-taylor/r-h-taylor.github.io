WIE_F2015_12 · How to be a Successful Engineer, Fall 2015 · §3.p8

Slocum's pedagogical retelling: a German railroad engineer asks Hertz how much weight he can add to a locomotive before denting the tracks, leading Hertz to derive contact stress mathematics. Used to motivate the pen-to-temple in-class demonstration.

Now we're going to do an in-class experiment. The educationists tell us we need to do in-class hands-on learning. Get out your pen. Take the pointy end and put it up to your temple. Now push until it hertz. [Pun on Hertz.] Heinrich Hertz, one of the most famous mathematicians — the guy who actually invented frequency. That's why hertz is called hertz. His railroad friend in Germany said, I pull trains, and if I add weight to the locomotives I can pull more, but then I dent the tracks. How much weight can I put on? Now use reciprocity: instead of pushing with the pointy end, push with a round smooth end. Can you push harder before it hurts? He's the guy who figured out the math — that's why it's called Hertz contact stress analysis.
