MSE_F2017_01 · Materials Selection and Economics, Fall 2017 · §7.p1

Another economic externality is the Environmental Protection Agency. In 1980 it decided to do something about cleaning up sites. I remember in 1996 or so I was asked to go to a Pennzoil facility in Oil City, Pennsylvania, where a welder had been welding on a tank, and the tank blew up, threw the welder across the river, killed her, and caused a lot of damage. I remember going to that facility and looking at it, and this tank had been built in 1922. It was old riveted steel, they didn't even know how to weld in 1922. This facility had been built in the 1890s, and here in 1996, a hundred years later, it was still operating. They had different economics in the old days, and I'll show you a picture of some of that in a second. But I looked at it, and in the tank farm, where they have the big tanks and storing facilities, they had crushed stone around the tanks. If you went down one foot you would strike oil. They had been spilling oil for so long, a hundred years, that the ground was just nothing but oil underneath the stone. These were relatively small tanks because in the 1920s we didn't use great big tanks like we do today out of welded construction.
