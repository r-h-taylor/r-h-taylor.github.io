Helicopter blade intellectual property dispute

Appears in 1 lecture.

CS_F2012_10 · Codes and Standards, Fall 2012 · §6.p1

The lecturer's own consulting case. One party reverse-engineered another's rotor blade design; the question was whether the substituted aluminum alloy was "as good" by aerospace certification standards. Used to walk through fatigue testing methodology, S-N curve interpretation, confidence intervals, and the damage-tolerant design approach.

Helicopter blades. This is something I worked on. They were arguing essentially intellectual properties. Somebody was trying to copy somebody else, and the question was, was their design as good? Because there's a lot in reverse engineering. In aerospace, you have to show "as good" from every standpoint, otherwise you have to do a full qualification on your own, which is very difficult — it means a lot of actual up-in-the-air testing and analysis. You typically only have two of these blades.
