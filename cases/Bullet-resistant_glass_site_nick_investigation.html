MSE_F2016_10 · Materials Selection, Fall 2016 · §9.p1

Site visit where the client wanted to know whether nicks and impact indications in glass-clad polycarbonate panels meant their bullet-resistant glass was defective. Substitute used this to make the distinction: bullet-resistant ≠ impervious; a nick does not indicate manufacturing failure if the glazing still meets penetration-resistance and no-fragment criteria.

The last one I want to get to is bullet-resistant glass. I was at a site a few years ago where they wanted to know if there was an issue — they had some nicks in the glass, slight areas where the bulletproof glass had indications that something had hit it. They wanted to know what was wrong. One thing to note: bullet-resistant is not the same as saying the glass is impervious to any breakage. Professor Eagar showed you the bulletproof glass he had — it has multiple layers, about five or six different layers stacked up. Just because you're buying bulletproof glass doesn't mean you can't get scratches and breaks. They do bulletproof glass as a glass-clad polycarbonate — that allows the glass to absorb more energy. The requirement is that the glazing must resist penetration by a projectile, and you don't have glass fragments flying. If you shot a projectile at the glass and it stopped the bullet without shards everywhere, that's glass operating as intended. A nick on the glass doesn't mean it was manufactured improperly.
