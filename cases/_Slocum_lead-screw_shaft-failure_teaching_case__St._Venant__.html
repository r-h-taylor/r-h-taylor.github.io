WIE_F2015_12 · How to be a Successful Engineer, Fall 2015 · §2.p4

Slocum's go-to undergraduate teaching artifact: a motor-pulley-belt-leadscrew assembly where the catalog-engineered version places the belt load at 3–5 characteristic dimensions from the bearings, causing predictable shaft and bearing failure. Reciprocity (flipping the hub) puts the load near the bearings and fixes it.

[Slocum holds up a small lead-screw assembly.] Motor, shaft on motor, pulley on shaft, belt driving another pulley, to a screw that drives the nut, then a car to go. You have engineers who make this in the lab. Oh, this is beautiful, look, it's on the front page of the web page. And Alex goes — [gesture] — teleport. Why? Because they do the crack cocaine.
