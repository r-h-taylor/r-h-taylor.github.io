`Stainless steel pipes with bleach storage`

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

WM_S2014_04 · Welding Metallurgy, Spring 2014 · §6.p3

Balmforth's domestic example of chloride-induced stress corrosion cracking: "if you've got stainless steel pipes under your sink and that's where you store your bleach, sometimes it can be a problem." Used as the everyday illustration of the chloride-environment caution that runs through §6.p2.

Student: [Question about chloride environments.]

Yeah, anything with chloride in it. It's really a problem where there's some kind of stress, but when you've got welding you've always got some residual stress. So we often find when something has cracked or there's been a problem, it's called stress corrosion cracking. Professor Eagar, if he hasn't talked to you about that, will be talking to you about that in more detail.
