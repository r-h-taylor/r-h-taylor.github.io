`Roof joist collapse from poor welding during snow storms`

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

CS_F2012_14 · Codes and Standards, Fall 2012 · §7.p5

The guest's forensic experience: of nine roof-joist collapses he investigated over about a year, eight involved welding defects at the diagonal-to-chord connections. The teaching points are (1) the demand side of the design is well-specified by code (snow loads, drift loads, etc.), but (2) the capacity side depends on weld quality, which is variable in practice, and (3) the design choice of single-sided vs. two-sided welds, and of fillet welds vs. round-tube-to-flat-bar welds, materially affects both inspectability and redundancy.

What happens on the capacity side — and what I found out, of nine different collapses that I looked at over about a one-year period, there was only one job where they didn't have any welding issue. It's not because you couldn't make these connections strong enough; they were just not built strong enough. Now it doesn't mean that all the joists have low-quality welds, because obviously I went on to what came down primarily. We did have some inspection on existing structures that were not under distress. But when we talked about a failure in that specific industry, what I have seen is a lot of this.
