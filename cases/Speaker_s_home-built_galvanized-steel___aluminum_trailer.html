CS_Su2012_06 · Codes and Standards, Summer 2012 · §11.p2

Personal anecdote. Aluminum cover over galvanized steel frame proved hard to join cleanly. Lesson: think about joints before choosing materials.

You want to think about the joints very early on. A stupid example: I built a trailer that has all the upper sheet metal — the cover is aluminum — and my frame is galvanized steel. I can't really connect them easily together, I've got to put fasteners everywhere. If I did it again today, I'd probably do all aluminum. A little more expensive in material, but I wouldn't have that joining issue. When I started I wasn't sure I was going to put a cover, so it's kind of part of the process. When you're coming up with something new, a lot of things look like "oh yeah, we'll figure it out" — but which material, which material combinations, ideas of design, trying to avoid welds in critical locations: those come in very early on.
