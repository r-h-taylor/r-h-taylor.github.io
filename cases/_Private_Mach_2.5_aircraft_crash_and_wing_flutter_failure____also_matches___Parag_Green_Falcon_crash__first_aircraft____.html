`Private Mach 2.5 aircraft crash and wing flutter failure` *(also matches: `Parag Green Falcon crash (first aircraft)` — see note)*

WM_S2014_06 · Welding Metallurgy, Spring 2014 · §7.p2

Kit-aircraft Mach 2 program in Nevada desert; sold engines from Pratt & Whitney military jet derivative with movie-star customers assembling the airframe themselves under FAA self-build rules. First aircraft blown up at 40,000 ft, test pilot killed. Second aircraft crashed; Tom called in by the owner (the second test pilot). Theory: supersonic wing flutter from a poorly adhesive-bonded lead damping weight detaching. No mechanical fastener backing up the adhesive bond.

I'll tell you another story. Some people would like to own an F-15, go Mach 2 or something like that, and they're fairly rich. But the government won't sell you an F-15 without all kinds of export controls. Some movie stars and others have enough money, they could buy the engine. There was this one company — I don't know where they incorporated, but they were doing their tests in the Nevada desert, and it was called Pereg— Green Falcon [Peregrine Falcon]. I think they were selling these aircraft for like $10 million. Basically you just buy the engine from Pratt and Whitney or whoever, and it was the same engine basically as the commercial version. I don't know if I want to say it's the commercial version, because this one would go Mach 2. It was a military engine, but it was sort of a commercialized version of the military engine that's in the F-15.
