`Deep-water drill bit elastomer-free seal design (Slocum)`

WIE_F2015_12 · How to be a Successful Engineer, Fall 2015 · §5.p3

Slocum invented drill bits for hot deep drilling that eliminate rubber seals. His colleagues were hiring elastomer experts to make rubber that lasts at high temperature; Slocum's solution is to remove the rubber entirely. The bits enable horizontal drilling (and therefore fracking) by allowing hydraulic-signal directional control at high temperature.

We still need oil. I'm one of the inventors of revolutionary drill bits for hot deep drilling. I solved the problem of seals — when you drill on this propeller, you fish, you knew there'd be a problem with the seals. I got rid of the elastomers. The way you get horizontal drilling, which is where all the fracking comes from: the drill bit is rotating, and to go down, a little flapper pushes the bit down. To go left, as the drill rotates, a hydraulic signal pushes the valve to the left. But when you go deep, it gets hot, and the rubber seals die. The colleagues I was originally hired with were hiring the elastomer gods to figure out rubber that would last at high temperature. I figured out how to get rid of all the rubber. It works far better, doesn't wear out, and it's cheaper.
