Sir Alastair Pilkington and Kenneth Bickerstaff invented the float glass process between 1953 and 1957. Molten glass floats on a tin bath, producing uniform thickness and surface finish without polishing. Pilkington licensed the process widely and became wealthy; later family lawsuits. Float glass production: ~53 million tons/year.

Then the float glass process came around. This is an image from Pilkington. This really revolutionized the industry. The name comes from Sir Alastair Pilkington — between 1953 and 1957 he and Kenneth Bickerstaff invented the float glass process. It created high-volume applications and much better uniformity. What happens is you float the glass over a bath of molten tin. You have your molten tin, and it's almost like oil and water, okay. You have your molten glass and raw materials on top, and because of surface tension it spreads out and fills the whole region where your panels come off the line.
