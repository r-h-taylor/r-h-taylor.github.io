MSE_F2016_10 · Materials Selection, Fall 2016 · §5.p3

Mentioned as an example of multilayer-IGU walk-on glass; speaker calls it the "Chrysler Building" but means the Skydeck ledge at Willis Tower (formerly Sears Tower). Flagged in editorial register.

Exactly. One of the big issues with glass in buildings is that there are multiple panels — it's called an IGU, an insulating glass unit. Think of it as a sandwich structure. There's that glass bridge in China that's been in the news this past year, you can walk on it thousands of feet in the air. In Chicago they have the ledge at the top of the [Sears Tower — speaker said Chrysler Building] where you can look down through the glass. With those types of glasses they do three-layer IGU. On the China bridge, they had a fracture in one of the panels, and the reason was they didn't tell people walking on it that you couldn't wear high heels. One of the ladies had high heels and it impacted one panel and broke one of the layers. The news agencies and the bridge makers said there's no need to worry, we have redundancy. When they add these other layers, two-layer or three-layer IGU, it's for redundancy — you can lose one layer without somebody falling through. So they do consider these things in the design. But thermal stress is a very big factor in glass breakage. What are some other reasons you can get glass fracture?
