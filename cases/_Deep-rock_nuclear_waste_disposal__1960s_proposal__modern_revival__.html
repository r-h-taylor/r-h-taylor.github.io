`Deep-rock nuclear waste disposal (1960s proposal, modern revival)`

PROPOSED case — pending reconciliation against canon

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

WIE_F2015_12 · How to be a Successful Engineer, Fall 2015 · §6.p4

A 1960s proposal — drill 10,000 ft (2 km) into hard rock at the reactor site itself, store all spent fuel from that plant in a one-meter-diameter borehole at that depth. Slocum argues this was infeasible in the 1960s but is now feasible thanks to oil-industry deep-drilling technology (including his own bits). Pairs with supercritical CO2 reactor cycles that eliminate cooling water.

I'm a staunch fan of nuclear power if you do it properly. In the sixties it was proposed how to deal with nuclear waste. Unfortunately, nuclear power plants became political footballs — it's a collusion between unions and lawyers and environmentalists: if we just keep the lawsuits going long enough, the plant takes five times as long to build, more jobs for everybody for longer. The proper way is to find an area where the geologic strata is deep hard rock. Here's your nuke — you drill down 10,000 feet in hard rock, 2 km, one meter diameter, and in that zone you put all the spent nuclear fuel that will ever be created from this nuke. Instead of collecting it and shipping it all to Utah, or mixing with coal, or whatever they're going to put in Nevada, you send it back down to the Earth. It's so deep there's nothing down there — we know that from the petroleum industry. You couldn't drill this in the sixties. And the other key factor: new nuclear plants are going to run hotter with molten salt, so you don't need cooling water — supercritical CO2 cycles. We can build this now, with enough plants, as the way repositories should be built. Made possible by the oil industry and deep drill bits. It gets really hot, 300 to 400 °C down there.
