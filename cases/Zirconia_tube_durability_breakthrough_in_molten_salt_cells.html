Zirconia tube durability breakthrough in molten salt cells

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

SMS_F2013_13 · Structural Materials Selection, Fall 2013 · §5.p1

U.D. Pal's discovery that MgF2-CaF2 eutectic gives ~100 microns corrosion in 500 hours, enabling 5,000-hour tube lifetime targets and viable cost economics. Idea dates from late 1960s; Swiss Aluminium had early-1970s patents but couldn't make tubes last.

The main issue is the zirconia tubes. This idea has been around since the late '60s. If you look up in the patent literature, you see Swiss Aluminium has a couple of patents granted in the early '70s on basically this technology, because of all the advantages I mentioned. However, if your zirconia tube doesn't last very long, then your tube costs basically outweigh the advantages. So U.D. Pal's real breakthrough was a combination of chemical metallurgy thinking and stubborn persistence — he finally found molten salt combinations that don't destroy the zirconia. I can talk about it because he's at BU; he's published this. It turns out magnesium fluoride-calcium fluoride eutectic, unlike cryolite, does not attack the zirconia. In 500-hour tests you get about 100 microns of zirconia corrosion. So if we run for 5,000 hours as our target lifetime — that's a little more than 6 months — that's 1 millimeter of corrosion. If they last that long, the cost of zirconia tubes per kilogram of magnesium ends up being a very small fraction of our total cost. That's the enabling breakthrough.
