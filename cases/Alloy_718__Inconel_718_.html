Alloy 718 (Inconel 718)

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

WM_S2014_11 · Welding Metallurgy, Spring 2014 · §5.p1

Used as the principal example of an alloy developed for resistance to strain-age cracking, by virtue of being strengthened by gamma double prime (rather than gamma prime) precipitates and thus tolerating lower titanium and aluminum content.

A few ways to prevent strain-age cracking. You can select a resistant material. Alloy 718 was developed largely for this reason. It's strengthened by the gamma double prime strengthening precipitate rather than the gamma prime, so it's got lower titanium and aluminum. Research has shown that by lowering titanium and aluminum in these alloys, you can form alloys that are resistant to strain-age cracking. Alloy 718 falls down here — this is kind of the boundary between resistant and susceptible. By keeping your aluminum and titanium low, thereby not forming the gamma prime but forming the gamma double prime or some other strengthening precipitate, you can reduce the susceptibility to strain-age cracking.
