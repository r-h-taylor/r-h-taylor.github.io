Reheat cracking / strain age cracking

Appears in 1 lecture.

WM_S2014_10 · Welding Metallurgy, Spring 2014 · §2.p3

Post-weld aging promotes carbide precipitation along grain boundaries, leading to further cracking. Strain age cracking in nickel alloys is the analog.

Continuing on weldability of the martensitic alloys, as far as cracking goes: solidification cracking susceptibility is generally low in these alloys. The main concern as far as cracking is the hydrogen induced cracking, particularly for the higher carbon grades. Also, just because of the martensitic structure, you get poor mechanical properties as-welded — low ductility, low toughness. The problem with low ductility and low toughness is you can have catastrophic brittle failure of your component, and so you typically need to do some post weld stress relief to reduce that. There's also something called reheat cracking, which is basically after the structure has been welded, the post weld aging or reheating of the alloy can promote some carbide precipitation along grain boundaries and within the grains, which stresses the alloy and can lead to further cracking.
