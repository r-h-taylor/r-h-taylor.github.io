TQI_S2018_01 · Total Quality Improvement, Spring 2018 · §7.p3

Carnegie's $100K land purchase, 1921 opening, Taylor as industrial-management/industrial-engineering founder. Used to argue Harvard's engineering ambitions are old, not new.

In the meantime, Harvard didn't have an engineering school, but Andrew Carnegie had given them $100,000 to buy some land in Allston. The Supreme Court said they couldn't do it in 1917, and in 1921 they opened a building in Allston, and they called it — what's the Harvard building in Allston — it's called the Harvard Business School. Who was on the faculty? Frederick W. Taylor was one of the people on the faculty. He's known as the father of industrial management, also known as industrial engineering. Harvard never thought they had an engineering school. In fact, back in the 1980s when they started coming out with the US News & World Report — who's the number one school in this and that — after about five or six years, Harvard actually showed up number eight on the top engineering schools in the country. And Derek Bok was the president of Harvard, and someone says, well what do you think about Harvard showing up at number eight in engineering, because that's pretty good considering we don't even have an engineering school.
