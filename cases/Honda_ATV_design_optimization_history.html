Honda ATV design optimization history

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

CS_F2012_10 · Codes and Standards, Fall 2012 · §3.p2

Used to illustrate that statistical design is an after-the-fact discipline. Honda's initial ATVs were overdesigned for lack of failure-rate history; later iterations were optimized against accumulated field data. Connects to the previous lecture's ATV example (welds-not-needed-all-around).

That's why it's easier to do it after the fact, and that's why it's very hard to start from scratch with a very optimized design. When Honda started to do these little ATVs, they were clearly overdesigned. They were much stronger and much more long-lasting than they probably wanted them to be, but they wanted to penetrate the market, and they didn't have the history of all of this being used everywhere, and they were somehow able to afford it. The exhaust system, everything — things would just last forever. And they changed. They went back and started to optimize based on history. So I don't want to create the false idea that you'll start from scratch, take some notes, go to some handbooks and come up with a statistical design for your new product. You can do it in an existing product. And that's more what we're going to be doing for the next twenty minutes or so.
