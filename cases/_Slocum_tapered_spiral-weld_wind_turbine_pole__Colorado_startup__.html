WIE_F2015_12 · How to be a Successful Engineer, Fall 2015 · §6.p5

Slocum's Colorado startup. Standard wind turbine poles are constrained to 4.25 m max diameter (bridge clearance for shipping), forcing 3-inch wall thickness, multi-pass welding, premium cost. His insight: bring the *factory* to the site, fabricate tapered spiral-welded poles 7 m at base to 3 m at top, use one-inch steel, one-third the steel mass, lower wind energy cost by ~10% (the projected tipping point).

So we can solve the nuclear waste problem. Next: listen for the whining. Wind power, too expensive. Let's figure out why. There's plenty of data — you look at pie slices showing where the costs are. I look and say, okay, this is the biggest slice of pie — I'm a big guy, I do an insane amount of endurance marathons, I can eat whatever I want, what's that? And you look — and this is the pole. Thirty percent of the cost of a big wind turbine is the pole.
