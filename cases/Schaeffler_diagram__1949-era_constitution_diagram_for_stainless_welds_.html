Schaeffler diagram (1949-era constitution diagram for stainless welds)

Appears in 1 lecture.

WM_S2014_04 · Welding Metallurgy, Spring 2014 · §3.p4

the central pedagogical tool of the first half of the lecture. Balmforth uses it to locate austenitic, ferritic, martensitic, and duplex alloy regions, and to introduce the iso-ferrite line concept that returns in §7 and §8.

This diagram was developed a long time ago by a guy named Schaeffler. What it does is it predicts the microstructure of a given stainless steel. If you look at the axes, you've got a chromium equivalent along the x-axis and a nickel equivalent along the y-axis. What they've done is they've taken the various alloying elements that you might find in your stainless steel and they've made them equivalent to either chromium or nickel. In doing the research to develop this type of diagram, you have to determine for example on the nickel equivalent that carbon is about thirty times more potent in forming austenite than the nickel is, whereas manganese is about half as potent.
