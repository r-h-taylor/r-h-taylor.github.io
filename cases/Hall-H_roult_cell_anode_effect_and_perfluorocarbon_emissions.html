Hall-Héroult cell anode effect and perfluorocarbon emissions

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

SMS_F2013_13 · Structural Materials Selection, Fall 2013 · §4.p4

Powell explains the anode effect as a key advantage that Infinium's separated-anode design eliminates. CF4 has 6,500× the GWP of CO2; C2F6 about 9,000×.

This has a lot of advantages. It eliminates something called the anode effect, where if you get CO2 bubbles forming, you get very high current density at the anode, and then you don't just have oxygen reacting with the oxide ions, but fluoride ions, and you form perfluorocarbons. CF4 has about 6,500 times the global warming potential of CO2. C2F6 is about 9,000 times. This eliminates that because you don't have any fluorine in contact with the anode. The zirconia is very selective — it only allows oxygen through. It eliminates HF in the anode gas because the anode is in a separate chamber from the rest of this. It eliminates the graphite anode plant. You can capture the energy from this CO2 and water, or from this oxygen, where you really can't from the anode gas in a Hall cell plant because of the HF.
