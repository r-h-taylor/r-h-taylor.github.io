`Tomato greenhouse/warehouse weld failure`

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

CS_F2012_14 · Codes and Standards, Fall 2012 · §8.p2

A specific instance from the guest's forensic work, illustrating low-convexity fillet welds that pulled off the chord surface rather than ripping the parent metal. Used as a concrete example of the convexity / contact-angle teaching point.

That's a big warehouse for tomatoes — greenhouse — that I worked on. Same story, where you look at this weld and we talked a little bit about convexity. So maybe today you'd be able to say, if you go somewhere and you see this, oh, this looks like it's a little sort of deposited on the surface, as opposed to being really well fused. It has a low contact angle here. This is the same type of weld that, after the fact, you can see it just pull off from the surface of that chord, as opposed to ripping the metal apart with it.
