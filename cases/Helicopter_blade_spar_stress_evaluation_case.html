Helicopter blade spar stress evaluation case

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

CS_Su2012_06 · Codes and Standards, Summer 2012 · §9.p1

Worked example for fatigue-based safety factor design. Aluminum spar at leading edge, honeycomb core, aluminum skin. Centrifugal + uplift + drag combine to put peak tension under the nose. Strain-gauge instrumentation near the rotor characterizes the load spectrum. Worked example numbers: 30,000 cycles, 12 KSI design, 18 KSI at 1.5 safety factor.

The spar for a helicopter blade. What we're looking at is a cross section of that blade. It has an aluminum spar at the front, and then a honeycomb core structure with an aluminum skin all the way to the back. This could be about a foot across from tip to tail. If I draw that line from the rotor, you rotate at high speed, so you'll have the centrifugal force creating tension this way. The leading edge is right here — that's where the highest tension is going to be, because of the drag force. The air pushes the blade backward, creating a bending moment in the rotation direction. And then we also have uplift, otherwise the helicopter wouldn't be that helpful.
