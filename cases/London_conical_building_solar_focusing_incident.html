London conical building solar focusing incident

MSE_F2016_10 · Materials Selection, Fall 2016 · §6.p6

Substitute recounts a London building (unnamed, likely 20 Fenchurch Street / "Walkie-Talkie") whose curved glass curtain wall focused sunlight onto a spot across the street hot enough to melt car hoods. The same designer reportedly had this issue on two or three buildings. Used to illustrate that sunlight angle and building geometry can create unforeseen thermal stress loading on glass panels.

Sun lighting. There was a famous building in London a couple of years ago. The designer who designed it has had this issue on two or three buildings — he was known for conical buildings with glass curtain wall facades. What happened was, at one area across the street, two blocks down, they were getting temperatures so hot it was melting car hoods. Super hot. The design of the building acted like a lens or a mirror and focused the sunlight at one spot. There's a famous picture, Google it, somewhere in London. Now people know not to park in that area if they want to leave with the car later that day operating. The sun's angle affects your thermal stresses. People say, why do I have 50 broken panels on this side of the building and none on the back side? More often than not, when the sun comes up and hits it, you're getting thermal stress breakage. So sunlight can actually fracture glass panels.
