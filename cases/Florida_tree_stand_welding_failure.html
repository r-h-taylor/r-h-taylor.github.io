Florida tree stand welding failure

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

CS_F2012_09 · Codes and Standards, Fall 2012 · §13.p1

Tom's marquee interjection. Same teaching point as Dr. Delmar's trailer case (welds in compression vs. tension), but with a fatal outcome — Tom's case down in Florida where a tree stand failed because the weld was placed in tension. The convergence of teaching point between Dr. Delmar (designed for it; survived) and Tom (didn't; killed someone) is the lecture's strongest pedagogical moment.

Tom Eagar: This idea that you've got at the top, of putting the weld in compression — it's pretty fundamental. I had a case down in Florida where a guy — you talked about designing your trailer, well, I had the exact same thing in a tree stand and it killed the guy. Because someone didn't think about: if you have a choice, if the loading is going to be this way, you put the weld up here so it's in compression. You don't put it this way so that you're pulling right through the weld, okay. When you're loading this way, your two members are just pushing against each other, and base steel has less variation than the weld.
