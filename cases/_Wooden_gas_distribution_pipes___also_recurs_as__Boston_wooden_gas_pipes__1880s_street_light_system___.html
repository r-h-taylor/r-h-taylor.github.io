`Wooden gas distribution pipes` (also recurs as `Boston wooden gas pipes (1880s street light system)`)

Appearances across the corpus

CS_Su2012_07 · Codes and Standards, Summer 2012 · §4.p3

Gun-drilled wooden logs caulked with oakum, used for the 1880s street-light gas system before cast iron and steel.

So how come all through Boston they put in gas piping in the 1930s — I live in a house built during the Depression, and going down our street, gas piping was put in. What did they use before that for gas piping? They used wooden logs that were gun-drilled out, caulked up with some oakum, and then they got to cast iron pipe in the 1880s. But all they used gas for back then was streetlights. It was in the 1920s or so they started using steel piping. The pipe running down my street, water pipe and gas pipe, have been there more than 70 years. Why aren't they full of holes?
