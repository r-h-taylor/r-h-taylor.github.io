Solidification cracking in nickel-based alloys

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

WM_S2014_10 · Welding Metallurgy, Spring 2014 · §6.p1

Driven by liquid film wetting along solidification grain boundaries with low-melting eutectic phases (Laves phase, sulfur/phosphorus impurities). Mitigated via solution-annealed welding, joint design (convex fillets, stringer beads), multiple-pass welds, and resistant filler choice.

So, solidification cracking. Before I really talk about that, I wanted to discuss the various types of boundaries that are created in an austenitic steel or austenitic nickel base alloy as the material solidifies. What you've got is two solid regions coming together as the material solidifies. Remember I talked about the low melting phases that partition and are pushed along ahead of the solidification front — that will be the last to solidify. You've also got solidification cells or dendrites that are growing together, and as these fronts come together you get something called a solidification grain boundary, where the two solidification fronts meet and form a boundary.
