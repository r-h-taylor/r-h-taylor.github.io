Washington DC government steam piping drip leg connection failure

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

CS_Su2012_06 · Codes and Standards, Summer 2012 · §5.p4

Drip-leg end cap was welded in a geometry that put the highest tension at the root of the weld (rather than using a hemispherical cap that loads the wall and weld equally). Pressurization opened the joint at the worst location. Teaches: a code-mandated geometry exists for this reason; ad hoc repair geometry consumes safety factor; "no point in thick walls if the connection capacity is half of the wall capacity."

I've done another project on a steam piping system connecting in Washington DC. We didn't go to the White House, but it was supplying the whole government building area. We looked at a drip leg that was collecting condensate. So you have a line, and at the bottom of the line you have a jump between different sections. In the manhole you have a little relay that collects the condensate and redistributes it back to the system. The connection at that drip leg was built in an unusual way. It had the proper material thickness, but I'll show you the detail.
