Harvard attempts to acquire MIT (1873–1917) and Gordon McKay trust Supreme Court ruling

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

TQI_S2018_01 · Total Quality Improvement, Spring 2018 · §7.p1

Three Harvard attempts; 1914–1917 students received dual degrees; Massachusetts Supreme Court blocked the merger ruling McKay trust funds could not be used to purchase MIT.

But the next part of the story, for you business school guys, I'm going to tell you the origin of Harvard's engineering school. From 1873 to 1917, Harvard tried to purchase MIT three times. From 1914 to 1917, students were getting a degree from both institutions. They had merged, until the Supreme Court of Massachusetts — now we're getting into the law — said you can't use the Gordon McKay trust funds at Harvard. Gordon McKay was a wealthy merchant who gave Harvard ten million dollars. You can't use the Gordon McKay trust funds to purchase the land-grant college of Massachusetts, which was MIT. So they were shot down. I have also been told, but I haven't confirmed it, that the MIT faculty had a vote and turned it down. I don't think that's necessarily true since they had actually merged.
