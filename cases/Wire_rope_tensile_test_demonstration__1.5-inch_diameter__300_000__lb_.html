MSE_F2016_10 · Materials Selection, Fall 2016 · §1.p2

Substitute shows a video of a full-scale tensile test of 1.5-inch diameter braided steel wire rope in a three-story test frame. Tensile strength over 300,000 lb. Audible pings during loading are individual strands breaking — the braid design allows partial strand failure without assembly failure.

Before we get into glass, last week we got talking about wire rope, in particular steel wire rope, and I want to show you this video. This was a full-scale test on about one-and-a-half-inch diameter wire rope. The testing structure is about three stories high. The tensile strength on this was over three hundred thousand pounds — extremely strong, and some of the strength is added by the design, by the way they braid the wires. We'll just watch the end where the fracture occurs. If you hear some pings, those are individual strands of the rope breaking over time. The rope is designed so that you can break a couple of individual strands without the entire assembly failing.
