Martensitic stainless steels (410, 416, 420, 422, 440)

Appears in 1 lecture.

Appearances across the corpus

WM_S2014_10 · Welding Metallurgy, Spring 2014 · §1.p5

Standard alloys in the martensitic class. 410 as workhorse; 416 free-machining; 420 higher corrosion resistance.

Some of the standard alloys you'll find in martensitic stainless steels: probably the most common is 410, the workhorse alloy. 416 is free-machining — it's got a little more sulfur in it. 420 is a little more corrosion resistant — it's got a slightly higher chromium content. And then you've got other specialty alloys like 422 and 440. One of the limitations on martensitic stainless steels is the service temperature, only up to about 1200 degrees F or 650 degrees C, because you start to lose both corrosion resistance and mechanical properties above those temperatures.
