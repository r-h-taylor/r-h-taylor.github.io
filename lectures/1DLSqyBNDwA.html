A billion-dollar project. They have two Michelson interferometers — these are the 90-degree ones — you send the lasers with a laser, and then you get the beating frequency coming back if the two of them shorten in length. The engineering that goes into it to align those four-kilometer legs — they have two of them in Livingston, Louisiana, and two of them up in Hanford, Washington. People propose putting others on the moon, and putting some in Australia, because the more, it's like astrophysical telescopes. §6.p2

§7. Physics, engineering, and the cleaning of house at MIT [19:46]

§7.p1

The physics department was originally — or a lot of the School of Science was basically there to serve the needs of the engineering departments at MIT. School of Engineering is the eight-hundred-pound gorilla. The physics department was there to teach physics to the engineers. The chemistry department was there to teach chemistry to the engineers. Basically most other departments have been created to help teach the engineers, because this was mens et manus. If you read William Barton Rogers, it said it was to be a place where they taught the arts but they were doing it from the fundamentals. The fundamentals was the science. ◆
§7.p2

What happened in the early 1930s or about that time — they had some very practical physicists who were great mechanical people. Ever heard of Charles Stark Draper, Draper Labs? §9. The Great Engineering Achievements of the Twentieth Century [35:15]

§9.p1

The National Academy of Engineering at the turn of the twenty-first century came up with the great engineering achievements of the twentieth century. We need to look at these. I'm going to put them up so you can see them. Electrification — Edison and Westinghouse — making electricity available even in rural areas, which was a real challenge because economically you can't justify running the wires out to the farms. That's one of the externalities of engineering. Everyone in the city could benefit because in the cities you have enough customers that you could sell the electricity and make a profit, and you didn't have to run the wires that far. But to start giving it to the farmers — that's a lot of copper to get out to that farm. They had to work various ways, whether tax incentives for the companies or whatever, to get electrification out.

◆
§9.p2

It's still a problem in certain parts of the country. You might know what state probably has the biggest problem with not having enough rural electrification. Alaska. Too big to run those wires. They basically run diesel generators in their little towns. It might only be fifty people in a town and they run diesel generators, paying about a dollar a kilowatt-hour. Pretty pricey. I'm paying twenty cents a kilowatt-hour this year. Last year I think it was fifteen. They told me Saudi Arabia is giving me a break this year.

§9.p3

The automobile — obviously, people now can go places. The airplane. Safe and abundant water. If you went back to the nineteenth century, a lot of people were drinking some pretty bad water.
